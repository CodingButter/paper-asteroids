On a phone: turn it sideways, thumbstick to fly, tap anywhere to shoot

A desk, a microphone, and a lot of paper

My dad and I had been making games together since I was about fourteen. In December of 2009, when I was twenty, we sat down at his desk and made this one in Flash over about ten days. The name on the credits, Jelly Sandwitch, is the two of us squashed into one word: Jamie and Scott.

The idea was simple: it should feel like a kid at a table, pushing paper cutouts around and making the noises with his mouth. So that is exactly what we did. Every sound in this game is one of us. The rocket thrust is my dad. The heartbeat that speeds up as you clear the screen is the two of us each saying "hum" into a microphone, taking turns. The ships, the explosions, the rips of paper. All us.

The original Flash file sat untouched for seventeen years. In 2026 I dug it out, decompiled it, and rebuilt it to run in any browser, with the same physics numbers, the same art, and the same voices. Now it lives here, and now there is a world leaderboard.

For Dad

I had so much fun making this with you. I remember it as one of the best times of my life, and I want you to know that I have never forgotten it.

Thank you for the hours. For sitting next to me while I figured out why the ship would not turn, for recording rocket noises with a straight face, for coming back the next evening and the one after that. We bumped heads plenty (we always have) and you never once stopped showing up. That is the part I think about most now.

You took a curious kid and gave him a reason to keep going, and you were still doing it when that kid was twenty. Because you spent that time growing my interest in programming, I get to do what I love every single day. The skills, the stubbornness, the habit of sitting with a hard problem until it gives: all of that started at that desk with you. Everything I have built since is built on it.

It was a priceless time in my life. Thank you for always being there for me. This one is yours.

Love, Jamie

Every sound is us

No sound library. Two people, one microphone, and whatever noises we could make with our mouths. Press them.

The two hums are the heartbeat: they alternate, and speed up as the field clears. The rocket is my dad going shhhhhhhh for as long as he could. Hold the thrust in the game long enough and you will hear him run out of air, take a breath, and start again. A person cannot make a rocket noise forever. We left it in.
